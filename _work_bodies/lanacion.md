La Nación looked at how generative AI is changing advertising production in
Argentina, and asked me what actually changes. My first answer was that it is
not magic. A good final product still needs a director and a crew, maybe a
smaller one, with real pre and post production. What has changed is everything
before the shoot: days of storyboards and back and forth with the client now
go much faster, and we can show twenty versions of an idea before choosing one
to film.

Much of the content now is generated by one machine and read by another. When
generating costs almost nothing, the bottleneck moves from producing to
choosing. You can have fifty versions of an idea in ten minutes; the value is
knowing which one works and why, and no tool settles that. It is the argument
behind [my talk on why clients still need an agency](/work/agenciasargentinas/).

## What I said

The interview was in Spanish. These are my translations of the four quotes La
Nación ran, each with the article's own lead-in.
{: .body-note}

Rafael Quijano of Don by Havas put AI productions at 40 to 50% cheaper, as
long as the traditional roles are kept. The article noted that some warn of
agencies going cheaper still by dropping those roles altogether, and quoted me
on that:
{: .quote-context}

> “For the final product to be good, you need to hire a director and a crew,
> maybe a smaller one, but you still need pre and post production processes,
> and so on. It's not magic.”

For those still producing ads the traditional way, it said, AI has also cut
pre-production down a great deal:
{: .quote-context}

> “What used to be days of storyboards and back and forth with the client now
> gets resolved much faster. We can show twenty versions of an idea before
> deciding which one we shoot.”

It added my point that better decisions get made as a result, before anything
is spent on a shoot, and that it makes room for variants that were not worth
producing before, for different markets, formats and languages.
{: .quote-context .quote-after}

Looking ahead, after Matías Lafalla of GUT imagined agents working day and
night on the less important decisions, I pictured a future where content is
seen not only by people but also by machines:
{: .quote-context}

> “Today you already have ads, campaigns and articles generated by AI, and at
> the same time you have AI consuming that content. We are entering a moment
> where a good part of the content is generated by one machine and read by
> another.”

Several of the people interviewed agreed that AI will amplify people rather
than replace them. Quijano pointed out that among identical AI promotions, the
corner shop with a hand-lettered sign on cardboard still stands out. I agreed,
and said which human skill becomes the priority:
{: .quote-context}

> “What takes center stage is judgment. When generating costs almost nothing,
> the bottleneck stops being producing and becomes choosing. Today you can have
> fifty versions of an idea in ten minutes; the value is in knowing which of the
> fifty works and why. The tool doesn't solve that.”
